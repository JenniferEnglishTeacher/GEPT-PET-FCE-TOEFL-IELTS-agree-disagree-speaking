Discussion 07 — Should sports be mandatory for all college and university students? 大學應該把體育列為全體學生的必修嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are looking at student health. Surveys show that many university students sit for more than eight hours a day and rarely exercise, and stress and anxiety are becoming more common on campus. Some universities therefore require every student to take sports or physical education classes in order to graduate, while others leave the choice to students. In your opinion, should sports be mandatory for all college and university students? Why or why not?

David: I believe sports should be mandatory. Many students spend long hours sitting in lectures or studying in libraries, which can lead to stress, poor posture and health problems. Regular sports help students relieve stress, improve their concentration and build healthy habits that last into adulthood.

Mary: I disagree. College students already face heavy academic pressure, and forcing everyone to play sports may increase stress rather than reduce it. Not all students are interested in sports, and some prefer other ways to stay healthy, such as walking or yoga. Universities should respect personal choice.

Discussion 15 — Should schools start later in the morning so that students can get more sleep? 學校應該延後早上的上課時間，讓學生睡得更飽嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are examining sleep and student health. Research suggests that teenagers naturally fall asleep late and need eight to ten hours of sleep, yet many secondary schools begin before 8 a.m., so a large number of students come to class tired. Some schools have therefore moved their first lesson to 9 a.m. or later, while others say the traditional timetable works well. In your opinion, should schools start later in the morning so that students can get more sleep? Why or why not?

David: I think schools should start later. Teenagers' body clocks make it hard for them to fall asleep before 11 p.m., so an early start means they never get enough rest. Students who lack sleep cannot concentrate or remember what they learn. If the first lesson began at 9 a.m., they would be more alert, and their grades would improve.

Mary: I don't think that is a good idea. If school starts later, it also finishes later, which leaves less time for sports, clubs, homework and family dinner. Many parents begin work early and would find it difficult to take their children to school. Besides, students would probably just go to bed even later, so they would not actually sleep more.

She used to skip breakfast and doze off in class, but now she eats properly and arrives alert.

However, this is not necessarily true. Studies have found that when schools delay their start times, bedtimes stay almost the same, so students do sleep longer.
