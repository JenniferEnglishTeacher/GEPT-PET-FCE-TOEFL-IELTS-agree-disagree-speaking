Discussion 01 — Should STEAM courses be mandatory for all university students? 大學應該把 STEAM 課程列為全體學生的必修嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are discussing how universities should prepare students for a world shaped by artificial intelligence. Some universities now require every student, whatever their major, to take STEAM courses (science, technology, engineering, arts and mathematics). Supporters say that all graduates need technological literacy, while critics argue that students should be free to concentrate on their own field. Should STEAM courses be mandatory for all university students? Why or why not?

David: I think STEAM courses should be mandatory. AI now affects every job, so students in all majors need basic scientific and technological literacy to understand how it works. These courses also train problem-solving and creativity, which employers value in the modern workforce.

Mary: I disagree with David. Universities should respect academic diversity and personal interests. Not every student plans to work in a technology-related field, and required STEAM courses take time away from major-specific learning. Besides, critical thinking and ethics can be developed through humanities courses too.

Discussion 09 — Should governments prioritize spending on school facilities over curriculum quality? 政府的教育經費應該優先投入學校設施，而不是課程品質嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are looking at education budgets. Public money is limited, so governments have to decide what to fund first. Some people believe that more should be spent on school facilities and infrastructure, such as safe buildings, air conditioning, science labs and computers. Others think the money should go to improving the quality of the curriculum and to training teachers. If a government cannot afford both, should it prioritize school facilities over curriculum quality? Why or why not?

David: I think facilities should come first. Students cannot learn well in buildings that are overcrowded, falling apart or too hot in summer. We should fix the basics, which means safe and comfortable schools, before we worry about the details of the curriculum.

Mary: I disagree with David. A fancy new building does not teach anyone anything by itself. What really matters is what is taught and how well it is taught. A better curriculum benefits students everywhere, even in older buildings.

Discussion 17 — Should students be given grades? 學校應該給學生打成績嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are talking about how schools assess learning. Most schools give students grades, such as letters, numbers or percentages, for their tests and assignments. Supporters say that grades are a clear way to measure progress and to motivate students. Critics argue that grades focus too much on performance and do not give an accurate picture of what a student can really do, so some schools have replaced them with written feedback. In your opinion, should students be given grades? Why or why not?

David: I believe students should be given grades. Grades are the clearest way for students to understand how well they are doing and to see which areas they need to improve. Moreover, grades prepare students for the real world, where adults are judged on their performance at work.

Mary: I don't think students should be given grades. Grades create a needlessly competitive environment and make students feel that they are only valued for their scores, so many of them get discouraged. Instead, teachers should give personal feedback that explains what to improve and how to do it.

Supporters say that uniforms create a sense of equality and help students concentrate on their studies. Critics argue that uniforms limit students' freedom to express themselves and can be uncomfortable or expensive, and in recent years some schools have relaxed their dress codes. In your opinion, should students be required to wear school uniforms? Why or why not?

David: I think students should be required to wear uniforms. Uniforms reduce the visible economic differences between students, so nobody is teased or bullied because of cheap clothes or the lack of brand names. They also save time every morning, because students do not have to decide what to wear.

Mary: I don't think uniforms should be required. Teenagers are developing their own identities, and clothing is one of the main ways in which they express their personalities. Besides, many uniforms are uncomfortable, especially in hot weather, and students learn better when they feel comfortable.

In many countries, students spend two or three hours on homework every evening after a full day at school. Some educators argue that homework causes stress and brings few real benefits, and a number of schools in Europe and North America have already stopped assigning it. Others believe that regular practice at home is essential for learning. In your opinion, should schools abolish homework? Why or why not?

David: I think homework should be abolished. After seven or eight hours of lessons, students are exhausted, and more work in the evening only causes stress and takes away their sleep. Children need free time to rest and to be with their families. If lessons are taught well, students can do all the practice they need during school hours.

Mary: I disagree. Homework helps students remember what they learned in class; without practice, it is easy to forget new vocabulary and math rules. As the saying goes, practice makes perfect. Homework also teaches students to manage their time and to work on their own, and these skills will be very useful when they go to university.

Discussion 41 — Can online learning replace traditional classroom education? 線上學習能取代傳統的課堂教學嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are looking at how technology is changing the way people study. During the pandemic, millions of students attended lessons from home, and today many universities offer complete degree programs on the internet. Some experts predict that online courses will soon take the place of physical classrooms because they are cheaper and more flexible. Others insist that students learn best when they are in the same room as their teachers and classmates. In your opinion, can online learning replace traditional classroom education? Why or why not?

David: I believe online learning can replace the traditional classroom. Students can study anywhere and at any time, so they save hours of travelling to school every week. They can also pause a recorded lesson and watch a difficult part again until they understand it. In a normal classroom, the teacher explains something once and then moves on.
