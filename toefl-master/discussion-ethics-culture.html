Discussion 08 — Is targeted online advertising ethical, or is it an invasion of privacy? 針對個人的網路廣告合乎道德，還是侵犯隱私？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): Today, we are discussing the ethics of targeted advertising. Many websites and apps collect personal information, such as our search history, location and shopping habits, and use it to show each person different advertisements. Some people argue that this is an invasion of privacy. Others argue that it is simply an acceptable way to reach consumers with products and services they are interested in. What is your view? Is targeted advertising ethical, or is it an invasion of privacy? Why?

David: I think targeted advertising is ethical. It is a more efficient way to reach consumers with products and services that are relevant to their interests, and with the money companies save on marketing, they can afford to offer lower prices. Besides, we can always opt out by adjusting our privacy settings. As long as we have a choice, targeted advertising can be beneficial.

Mary: I disagree. Targeted advertising is an invasion of privacy. Advertisers should not be able to track us and use our personal information to sell us products. It is unfair to consumers and a violation of our rights. Instead, advertisers should focus on creating high-quality ads that appeal to a broad audience, which would also show respect for their customers.

Discussion 16 — Should artificial intelligence be used in military operations? 人工智慧應該被用於軍事行動嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are looking at the ethics of artificial intelligence in warfare. Armed forces around the world are already using AI to analyze battlefield data, guide drones and defend against cyberattacks. Supporters say that this technology protects soldiers and makes military operations more precise. Critics, however, worry about machines that make life-and-death decisions and about who is responsible when they make mistakes. In your view, should artificial intelligence be used in military operations? Why or why not?

David: I believe AI should be used in the military because it can reduce human casualties and strengthen national security. AI-powered systems can analyze battlefield data far faster than humans and respond more quickly in dangerous situations. For example, autonomous drones can carry out reconnaissance missions in hostile areas without putting soldiers' lives at risk.

Mary: I strongly disagree. Although AI may improve efficiency, it raises serious ethical and security concerns. Autonomous weapons could make life-and-death decisions without human judgment, and a machine might mistake a civilian for a target. Moreover, if these systems were hacked or malfunctioned, the consequences could be catastrophic. War is already morally complex, and removing human control would make it even more dangerous.

Discussion 24 — Is it ethical to keep wild animals in zoos? 把野生動物養在動物園合乎道德嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are looking at the ethics of how humans treat animals. Zoos are among the most popular attractions in the world, and millions of families visit them every year. Supporters say that modern zoos protect endangered species and teach the public about wildlife. Critics, however, argue that it is wrong to keep wild animals in captivity for human entertainment. In your opinion, is it ethical to keep wild animals in zoos? Why or why not?

David: I think keeping animals in zoos is ethical as long as they are well cared for. Modern zoos play an important role in conservation. Many endangered species, such as the giant panda, have been saved from extinction through zoo breeding programs. Animals in zoos also receive regular food and medical care, so they often live longer than they would in the wild.

Mary: I disagree. Wild animals are meant to live freely in their natural habitats, not in cages or small enclosures. Elephants and polar bears, for example, travel long distances every day in the wild, and in captivity they often show signs of stress and boredom, such as walking in circles. I don't think we have the right to take away an animal's freedom just so that we can look at it.

Discussion 32 — Should historic buildings be preserved even if this slows down urban development? 即使會拖慢都市發展，也應該保存歷史建築嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are discussing how cities balance culture and progress. As cities grow, they need more housing, offices and transportation, and old buildings are often torn down to make room for them. Some people believe that historic buildings are an essential part of a city's cultural heritage and must be protected, even if this slows down development. Others argue that cities should put the needs of today's residents first. In your opinion, should historic buildings be preserved even if this slows down urban development? Why or why not?

David: I believe historic buildings should be preserved. They are a physical record of a city's history, and they give residents a sense of identity. Once an old temple or a traditional street is demolished, it is gone forever, and no modern copy can replace it. A city without its old buildings looks just like every other city in the world.

Mary: I see it differently. Cities are for the people who live in them now, and many of those people cannot find an affordable home. Old buildings are often unsafe in earthquakes and very expensive to repair and maintain. I think it is enough to protect a few of the most important landmarks, so that the rest of the land can be used for modern housing and public transportation.

Discussion 40 — Is globalization a threat to local cultures and traditions? 全球化對在地文化與傳統是一種威脅嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are discussing culture in a connected world. Thanks to global trade, travel and the internet, people in different countries now watch the same films, wear the same brands and eat at the same restaurant chains. Some people worry that this process, known as globalization, is destroying local cultures and traditions. Others believe that it helps cultures to spread and grow. In your opinion, is globalization a threat to local cultures and traditions? Why or why not?

David: I think globalization is a real threat to local cultures. International chains and global brands are pushing out small traditional businesses, and city centers around the world are starting to look the same. Young people are also more interested in foreign pop music and fashion than in their own traditions. If this continues, many customs and crafts will simply disappear.

Mary: I disagree. Cultures have always changed and borrowed from each other, and that is what keeps them alive. Thanks to the internet, local artists and cooks can now share their traditions with a worldwide audience, so more people than ever know about them. Besides, people do not give up their own culture just because they enjoy foreign food or films.
