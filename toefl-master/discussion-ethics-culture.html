Discussion 08 — Is targeted online advertising ethical, or is it an invasion of privacy? 針對個人的網路廣告合乎道德，還是侵犯隱私？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): Today, we are discussing the ethics of targeted advertising. Many websites and apps collect personal information, such as our search history, location and shopping habits, and use it to show each person different advertisements. Some people argue that this is an invasion of privacy. Others argue that it is simply an acceptable way to reach consumers with products and services they are interested in. What is your view? Is targeted advertising ethical, or is it an invasion of privacy? Why?

David: I think targeted advertising is ethical. It is a more efficient way to reach consumers with products and services that are relevant to their interests, and with the money companies save on marketing, they can afford to offer lower prices. Besides, we can always opt out by adjusting our privacy settings. As long as we have a choice, targeted advertising can be beneficial.

Mary: I disagree. Targeted advertising is an invasion of privacy. Advertisers should not be able to track us and use our personal information to sell us products. It is unfair to consumers and a violation of our rights. Instead, advertisers should focus on creating high-quality ads that appeal to a broad audience, which would also show respect for their customers.

Discussion 16 — Should artificial intelligence be used in military operations? 人工智慧應該被用於軍事行動嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are looking at the ethics of artificial intelligence in warfare. Armed forces around the world are already using AI to analyze battlefield data, guide drones and defend against cyberattacks. Supporters say that this technology protects soldiers and makes military operations more precise. Critics, however, worry about machines that make life-and-death decisions and about who is responsible when they make mistakes. In your view, should artificial intelligence be used in military operations? Why or why not?

David: I believe AI should be used in the military because it can reduce human casualties and strengthen national security. AI-powered systems can analyze battlefield data far faster than humans and respond more quickly in dangerous situations. For example, autonomous drones can carry out reconnaissance missions in hostile areas without putting soldiers' lives at risk.

Mary: I strongly disagree. Although AI may improve efficiency, it raises serious ethical and security concerns. Autonomous weapons could make life-and-death decisions without human judgment, and a machine might mistake a civilian for a target. Moreover, if these systems were hacked or malfunctioned, the consequences could be catastrophic. War is already morally complex, and removing human control would make it even more dangerous.
