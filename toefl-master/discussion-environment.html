Discussion 04 — Should governments prioritize environmental protection over economic growth? 政府應該把環境保護看得比經濟成長更重要嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are looking at environmental policy. Economic growth creates new jobs and gives people money that they can use to improve their lives. On the other hand, a healthy environment can be enjoyed by both ourselves and future generations. These two goals often seem to conflict, for example when a government must decide whether to approve a new factory or protect a forest. If a government had to choose, should it prioritize environmental protection over economic growth? Why or why not?

David: I think the environment should come first. Economic growth is important, but not at the expense of the planet. Governments should shift towards more environmentally friendly practices, such as investing in renewable energy like solar and wind power. We will all live much healthier lives if the air, water and land around us are clean.

Mary: I understand David's point, but I believe economic growth has to be the priority. We need a strong economy to create jobs, reduce poverty and improve standards of living. Not only that, but when companies grow stronger and more profitable, they can afford to develop new technologies that solve our environmental problems.

Discussion 12 — Should people eat less meat in order to protect the environment? 人們應該為了保護環境而少吃肉嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are examining how our everyday choices affect the planet. Raising animals for food requires large amounts of land, water and animal feed, and it produces a significant share of the world's greenhouse gas emissions. For this reason, some experts encourage people to cut down on meat, especially beef. Others doubt that changing individual diets can make a real difference. In your view, should people eat less meat in order to protect the environment? Why or why not?

David: I think people should definitely eat less meat. Producing beef uses far more land and water than growing vegetables or beans, and cattle release large amounts of greenhouse gases. If millions of people replaced meat with plant-based meals just a few times a week, the total effect on the climate would be significant.

Mary: I see David's point, but I don't think our diets are the real problem. Most emissions come from power plants, factories and transport, so governments should focus on cleaner energy instead. Besides, meat is an important source of protein, and many farmers depend on raising animals to make a living.

Discussion 20 — Should private cars be banned from city centres? 應該禁止私家車進入市中心嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are looking at how cities can become cleaner and more liveable. In many large cities, traffic congestion and air pollution are getting worse every year. As a result, some cities, such as Oslo and Amsterdam, have closed parts of their centres to private cars. Others worry that such a ban would hurt local businesses and make daily life inconvenient for residents. In your opinion, should private cars be banned from city centres? Why or why not?

David: I believe private cars should be banned from city centres. Petrol and diesel vehicles that are stuck in traffic produce toxic emissions, which seriously damage people's health. Cities that have reduced car access have seen their air quality improve significantly. With fewer cars on the road, buses and trams could also run faster and more reliably.

Mary: I understand David's concern, but I think a complete ban goes too far. Many people, such as parents with young children and delivery drivers, depend on their cars every day. Besides, if customers cannot drive to the centre, the shops and restaurants there may lose business to shopping malls in the suburbs.

Discussion 28 — Should countries rely on nuclear energy to fight climate change? 各國應該依靠核能來對抗氣候變遷嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are examining how countries can reduce their carbon emissions while still meeting the growing demand for electricity. Nuclear power is one of the most controversial options. Some governments are building new reactors because they produce very little carbon dioxide, while others, such as Germany, have closed all of their nuclear plants because of safety concerns. In your opinion, should countries rely on nuclear energy to fight climate change? Why or why not?

David: I think nuclear energy should be part of the solution. A nuclear power plant produces almost no greenhouse gases while it is running, so it is far cleaner than coal or natural gas. If we are serious about cutting emissions quickly, we cannot afford to reject such a powerful low-carbon source of electricity.

Mary: I disagree. The accidents at Chernobyl and Fukushima showed how dangerous nuclear power can be, and one mistake can make an area unsafe for decades. In addition, nobody has found a truly safe way to store radioactive waste, which stays harmful for thousands of years. We should not leave this problem to future generations.

Discussion 36 — Should governments ban single-use plastic products? 政府應該禁用一次性塑膠製品嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are looking at plastic pollution. Every year, millions of tons of plastic waste end up in rivers and oceans, and much of it comes from single-use items such as bags, straws and cups, which are used for a few minutes and then thrown away. Some governments have already banned or restricted these products, while others prefer to leave the choice to consumers and businesses. In your opinion, should governments ban single-use plastic products? Why or why not?

David: I think governments should ban them. Single-use plastics take hundreds of years to break down, and they kill countless sea turtles, seabirds and fish that swallow them or get trapped in them. Asking people to use less plastic voluntarily has not worked, so a clear law is the only way to reduce plastic waste quickly.

Mary: I don't think a ban is a good idea. Plastic is cheap, light and hygienic, so small businesses such as food stalls and drink shops depend on it, and the alternatives cost much more. Besides, the real problem is not plastic itself but the fact that people litter and do not recycle. Governments should improve recycling instead.
