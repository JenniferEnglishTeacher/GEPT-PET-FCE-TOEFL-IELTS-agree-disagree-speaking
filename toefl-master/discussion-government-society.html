Discussion 05 — Should governments tax unhealthy products such as sugary drinks and junk food? 政府應該對含糖飲料、垃圾食物等不健康產品課稅嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are discussing public health policy. Many governments are considering whether to tax unhealthy products, such as sugary drinks and junk food. On one hand, taxing these products could discourage people from consuming them and reduce health problems. On the other hand, some argue that such taxes unfairly target lower-income families, who may rely on these products as affordable sources of food. If you had to choose, would you support or oppose a tax on unhealthy products? Why?

David: I support the idea of taxing unhealthy products. They are often high in sugar, salt and fat, which can lead to serious health problems such as obesity, diabetes and heart disease. By taxing them, the government can discourage people from consuming them. Moreover, the revenue can be used to fund campaigns that promote healthy eating habits.

Mary: I believe that such taxes are unfair to families that do not have a lot of money. Wealthy people will not care about the taxes, so their habits will not change. In addition, when prices go up, people usually try to save money by buying cheaper, lower-quality products, which could cause even more health problems. There are better solutions than taxes.

Discussion 13 — Should the voting age be lowered to 16? 投票年齡應該降到 16 歲嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are looking at how democracies decide who is allowed to vote. In most countries the voting age is 18, but Austria, Scotland and a few other places now allow 16-year-olds to vote in some or all elections. Supporters say that this gives young people a voice in decisions that will shape their future, while critics doubt that teenagers are ready for such a responsibility. In your view, should the voting age be lowered to 16? Why or why not?

David: I think the voting age should be lowered to 16. At that age, many young people already work part-time and pay taxes, yet they have no say in how that money is spent. Decisions about education, climate change and public debt will affect them for much longer than they will affect older voters, so it is only fair to let them vote.

Mary: I disagree. Most 16-year-olds are still in high school and have very little experience of real life, such as renting a home or supporting a family. They are also easily influenced by their parents, their teachers and what they see on social media. I think people should wait until 18, when they are legally adults, before they vote.

Discussion 21 — Should governments spend large amounts of public money on space exploration? 政府應該把大筆公帑花在太空探索上嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are discussing how governments decide what to spend public money on. Countries such as the United States, China and India invest billions of dollars every year in space programmes, including missions to the Moon and Mars. Supporters say that this research leads to new technology and inspires young people, while critics argue that the money should be used to solve urgent problems on Earth. In your opinion, should governments spend large amounts of public money on space exploration? Why or why not?

David: I believe space exploration is worth the cost. Many things we use every day, such as GPS, weather forecasts and satellite communications, exist because of space research. Besides, the money is not thrown away in space. It pays the salaries of scientists and engineers here on Earth and leads to technology that benefits everyone.

Mary: I see it differently. Millions of people still lack clean water, decent housing and basic health care. It is hard to justify sending a rocket to Mars while hospitals and schools at home are short of money. Governments should take care of their own citizens first and leave space travel to private companies.

Discussion 29 — Should governments use public money to fund the arts and culture? 政府應該用公帑資助藝術與文化嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are discussing how governments decide what to spend public money on. Many governments give financial support to the arts and culture, for example by funding museums, theatres, orchestras and local festivals. Supporters argue that the arts bring benefits to the whole of society that the market alone cannot provide. Critics, however, believe that taxpayers' money should be spent on more urgent needs, and that artists should rely on ticket sales and private donations. In your opinion, should governments use public money to fund the arts and culture? Why or why not?

David: I think governments should definitely fund the arts. Museums and theatres preserve our cultural heritage, and many of them could not survive on ticket sales alone. Public funding also allows artists to take creative risks, and it gives a voice to minority cultures that commercial companies usually ignore because they are not profitable.

Mary: I don't think public money should be spent on the arts. Governments have limited budgets, and hospitals, schools and housing are far more urgent. Besides, popular art can support itself. If people really value a concert or an exhibition, they will be willing to pay for it, and wealthy donors or companies can sponsor the rest.

Discussion 37 — Should governments require all young people to do one year of community service? 政府應該規定所有年輕人從事一年的社區服務嗎？

Time 時間：10 minutes ｜ Target 目標字數：at least 100 words（samples: about 150）

Professor (Dr. Miller): This week we are discussing the responsibilities that citizens have towards their society. Some politicians have proposed that every young person should complete one year of community service after finishing secondary school, for example by helping in hospitals, caring for the elderly or cleaning up the environment. Supporters say that this would build character and benefit society, while critics see it as an unfair burden on young people. In your opinion, should governments require all young people to do one year of community service? Why or why not?

David: I support this idea. A year of community service would teach young people responsibility and empathy, which they cannot learn from textbooks. Mary: I disagree. Forcing people to volunteer makes no sense. When service is compulsory, many participants feel resentful and do only the minimum. It would also delay young people's education and careers by a whole year, which is especially unfair to students from poorer families who need to start earning money as soon as possible.
